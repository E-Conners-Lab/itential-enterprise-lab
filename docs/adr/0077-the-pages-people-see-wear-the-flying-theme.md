# 0077 — The pages people see wear the flying theme, and every approval is a branded page

- **Status:** proposed (drafted 2026-10-07 for the owner's go)
- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Amends:** ADR 0072 amendment (the outage card's HTML page), ADR 0073 (the fabric cards), ADR 0068 (the branded
  portal page and Hand Off's approval), ADR 0044 (the approval tasks)
- **Related:** ADR 0075 (what the cards show: the agent's account, the independent check), ADR 0070 (the portal on
  production), the memory note `theme-aviation-and-fresh-captures` (owner decision, 2026-10-07)

## Context

Everything a person sees from this lab is the lab's own HTML: three Work Center cards generated by
`itential/workflows/build.py` (the AWS VPN outage card, the fabric BGP outage card, the BGP drill card; one shared
`card_page()` and one CSS block, `OUTAGE_CARD_CSS`) and the branded engineer page `itential/portal/deploy-aws-vpn/`.
All four wear the same nautical look: an anchor mark, a paper-and-ink palette with a cobalt action block, a "sea"
chart with a shore and waves on the portal. Five more approvals are not HTML at all: Deploy AWS VPN ("Approve the AWS
change"), Hand Off AWS VPN ("Approve the router change"), Push Configuration with Revert ("Approve the change under a
revert timer") and Tear Down's two cards are `ViewData` tasks that show a message and a raw JSON object with Approve
and Reject buttons. The owner decided (2026-10-07) that the next LinkedIn piece makes the customisation point: these
pages are the lab's own, generated and rendered in Work Center, and a customer can change them. Every capture made
under the old look is archived, never reused. The new captures start only after the knowledge corpus is live (it is:
57 scenarios, lab #143) and after this theme ships.

## Decision (owner, 2026-10-07; details for the owner's go)

1. **Visual only.** The flying theme changes the mark, the palette, the typography and the band. The brand line stays
   "Elliot's Itential Lab" with its role word after it ("outage response", "network automation portal"). No callsign,
   no persona. The structure, the labels, the exact lines, the independent-check badge, the agent's account, the
   approve and reject buttons and the note field are unchanged, and a test proves it: the same fixture renders to the
   same text when the markup is stripped, before and after.
2. **Vocabulary stays plain network engineering.** One phrase is allowed where it is already universal: Itential's own
   reads on a card may be labelled "pre-flight checks". Nothing else from the cockpit ("squawk", "go-around",
   "cleared", "wheels up") appears anywhere a person has to read.
3. **The palette: a night cockpit lit by its instruments.** The page is night ink (`#0B1322`), panels a shade lighter
   (`#121D31`) with a hairline edge, text a cool white (`#E6EDF7`) and a muted secondary (`#9FB0C8`). The three
   meaning colours keep their meanings and move to instrument tones: green `#39C27A` for Established, agrees, passed;
   amber `#F2B134` for waiting, pending, a drill's timer; red `#FF5A5F` for down, refused, disagrees. The action colour
   (the approve block, links, the active waypoint) is display cyan `#4FA3F7`. Contrast is checked for body text and
   for every meaning colour on the night ink (WCAG AA or better) before the PR.
4. **The mark.** An attitude indicator: a rounded square split by a horizon into sky (cyan) and ground (amber brown),
   with the white aircraft reference symbol (centre dot, wings, two short pitch ticks). Drawn once as inline SVG in
   `card_mark()` (data URI, since Work Center strips `<svg>`) and once in the portal page. It replaces the anchor
   everywhere; no other imagery is added.
5. **Typography.** The display face stays a condensed grotesque (system stack, so Work Center and the headless renders
   agree) and every reading, timer, address and counter is set with tabular numerals in the mono stack, the way
   instrument readouts are. Letter-spacing on the small caps labels widens a little.
6. **The band.** Same grid, same contents (brand, headline, lede, the waiting pill, the outage's age). The pill is amber
   on night ink; the age is a readout in tabular numerals. A one-pixel horizon line under the band carries the
   theme without adding content.
7. **The drawings follow the palette.** The topology and session drawings (`outage_card_topology`,
   `fabric_card_drawing`) and the ledger colours take their hex values from module constants (`CARD_GREEN`,
   `CARD_RED`, `CARD_AMBER`, `CARD_INK`, `CARD_TEXT`, `CARD_MUTED`) instead of literals, so the three meaning colours
   exist in exactly one place. The "broken tunnel" test (`t_col != green`) compares against the constant.
8. **Every approval is a branded page.** The five `ViewData` approvals (Deploy, Hand Off, Push Configuration with
   Revert, Tear Down step 1 and step 2) become `InteractiveHTML` tasks whose body is a `card_page()` built on the
   runner by one new pure function, `approval_card()`: the approval's message as the lede, its object laid out as a
   ledger (scalars) and code blocks (the block, the lines, the plan), the same Approve and Reject buttons, the same
   note field. The decision still comes back as the task's export; the success and failure edges are unchanged, so
   the tests that prove "nothing reaches the router before the approval" hold as they are. The "needs a look" and
   "drift" notices stay `ViewData`: they are notices for a person, not approvals, and carry no exact lines.
9. **The portal page.** Same page, same journey, same waypoint ids and classes (the JavaScript keys on them). The sea,
   the shore and the waves become a night flight-plan chart: a faint grid, a dashed great-circle route between the
   waypoints, the same labels. The hero copy is unchanged.
10. **Proof before the PR.** All seven pages are rendered headless (Chrome, scale 2) from the tests' own fixtures for
    the owner's look: the three generated cards, the five approval cards (Deploy, Hand Off, Revert, Tear Down 1 and 2;
    Deploy and Hand Off share one shape), and the portal page in preview mode. A test asserts the mark and the palette
    changed (no anchor path, no `#1F4FD1`, the new tokens present) and that the labels did not (the fixture text is
    identical with markup stripped). The three workflow documents are regenerated by `build.py`; the five approval
    workflows change too, because their approval task changes type.

## Consequences

- Deploy AWS VPN gains the input gate every other text-splicing workflow has (WEB-12): its approval message (the
  requester's note, the end time) is now rendered on the runner, so the note is checked first (no quote, backslash,
  newline or angle bracket; the public IP must be an IPv4 address; the other two fields are enums).
- Canvas only: the five helper tasks sit on a row above each approval; the layout engine places Hand Off's the same for
  every offset tried, so `tests/test_workflow_layout.py` allows 9 arrows through tasks there (was 5), under the owner's
  2026-10-03 rule that overlaps are fine when the workflow runs the same.
- One PR, visible on every card and on the portal; nothing an operator relies on moves or renames.
- The five converted approvals need a replay to production (platform-ha2-replay.yml) and one look at each in Work
  Center before the fresh drills. Hand Off's approval is on the AWS VPN path: the owner looks at the rendered card
  before any production Hand Off.
- The drawings' colours now come from constants, so a later palette change is a one-line edit per colour.
- The nautical look survives only in the archived captures (hub folders 1 to 17), which are kept, never reused.
- Not done here: converting the "needs a look" notices, adding web fonts (Work Center and the renders would differ),
  or any wording change beyond the one allowed phrase.
